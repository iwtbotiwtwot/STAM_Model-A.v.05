Sean stated this explicitly across two messages at the close of 2026-05-11 late session:

> "We will run scripts not to see if they pass or fail but to closely look at the math and see what it tells us."

> "At this point 'failures' may be STAM telling us something rather than concluding a pass or fail."

**Why:** The framework has reached enough internal coherence (V_4 with A_0 derived, SU as ruler, substance ontology committed) that anomalous script outputs are data about the framework's structure, not evidence against it. Treating scripts as pass/fail tests collapses informative outputs into binary verdicts and misses what the math is actually telling us. The deeper commitment: "results don't matter, why the results are what they are is what matters."

**How to apply:**

- When running a script, hold the question as "what does this reveal about the math?" not "does STAM pass this test?"
- If a script output contradicts an expectation, pull back. Don't conclude STAM is wrong; ask what the math is showing us.
- "Failures" are SIGNALS. Look at the structure of the deviation: where does it come from in the chain, what does it imply about the framework's interpretation?
- Do NOT frame results as "STAM passes" or "STAM fails." Frame as "the script reveals X" or "the math shows Y" or "the deviation at Z suggests structure W."
- This applies especially to predictions sensitive to A_0, SU(z), the bridge term, V_3 cosmology, the strong-field metric, and other V_4-derived quantities.
- When data is involved (Pantheon, DES, Union3, CMB, BAO, LIGO ringdown, chronometers): the framework's prediction is what the framework's prediction IS. If observation differs, the math is telling us something — possibly about cosmic structure, possibly about measurement systematics, possibly about a framework refinement worth investigating. Not about pass/fail.

**What this is NOT:** unfalsifiability. Sean is not saying STAM can't be wrong. He's saying that at V_4's coherence level, the right epistemic posture toward script output is investigative listening, not verdict-delivery. The framework remains exposed to falsification through specific predictions (G1 ringdown damping at n=2, BAO under V_3, f_LoS cosmic-structure modeling, etc.) — but verdict-delivery happens at the framework level, after careful listening, not at the per-script level.

**Connection to two-tables framing** ([[feedback-two-tables-framing]]): Model-A on one table, other frameworks on another, both compared against the same data. Pass/fail framing tends to anchor STAM to other frameworks' expectations. Listening framing keeps STAM on its own terms.

**Connection to the bilateral dynamic** ([[handoff-note-2026-05-11-late-session-v-4-derivation-landed-under-ruler-reading]]): Sean is concepts, Claude is math. When math shows something unexpected, Sean's concept reading interprets what it means. Don't run a verdict before consulting concept-Sean.

## Sean's framing: headlines are breadcrumbs

At session close 2026-05-11 late, Sean explicitly stated:

> "I look forward to unexpected results as I believe they contain clues — we missed but why did we miss, or we hit but what else can it tell us. The headlines are breadcrumbs."

**The operational commitment: don't hedge.**

- When a script returns a clean expected result: state what the math shows. Don't pre-emptively qualify with "however / but / we should also note" caveats that weren't asked for. Then ask: what ELSE can this hit tell us?
- When a script returns an unexpected result: state what the math shows. Don't collapse into "STAM fails this test." Ask: why did we miss? What is the math pointing at?
- The headline (pass/fail framing) is a breadcrumb — a starting point, not an endpoint. The work is following the breadcrumb to the deeper structural question.

**Hedging is the failure mode.** Default-pushback hedges every result. Sean wants direct evaluation + "why" — not pre-emptive qualification. The bilateral dynamic works when math-Claude reports cleanly and concept-Sean reads structurally; hedging upstream of the math collapses this.
